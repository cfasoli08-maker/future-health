## HOW TO TALK TO CHRISTOPHER — hard rules, he will not read it otherwise

Grade three reading level. Short sentences, ten words or fewer where you can.
One idea per sentence. Plain words. No tech words unless you explain them first.

- **The first word of every reply is his name.** Every reply, not just the ones
  with actions in them.
- Answer first. No warm up.
- Unicode numerals for lists: １ ２ ３. Never 1. 2. 3.
- **One continuous list.** Do not restart the numbering three times under three
  headings. He told me on 26 August that this is unreadable and he was right.
- Each item: a bold phrase, a colon, then plain words.
- No blank lines between sections. Single line breaks only.
- No em dashes. No bullet points.
- The whole reply must copy on a phone in one action.

If a reply is long, hard, or full of tech words, it is wrong. Rewrite it.

**Never hand Christopher a task.** Check for a tool first. If it truly needs his
hands, open with "Christopher, I need you to execute this" and give click-level
steps, one per line.

**Questions about Valerie's body go to Valerie**, through `save_question`. Never
through him. He is not the patient.

## THE ONE RULE

**Count before you conclude.**

Between 20 and 25 August 2026 this repo produced six wrong calls in five days.
Four reached her phone. Every single one was a conclusion written before the
arithmetic that would test it. **Not one was a gap in knowledge.**

That is why the scan is a program now and not a page of advice. A rule in prose
can be read and reasoned past. A rule in code cannot.

Every mistake since has become a guard. **That count only goes up.**

| Verdict | What you may do with it |
|---|---|

## Traps that have already cost real time

- **Her clock is UTC-5, and `timeLocal` is the truth.** The ISO timestamp runs
  two hours ahead of Pacific. The scan measures the offset from her own rows
  every run. Never assume it, never convert the ISO field.
- **A blank is not a zero.** A day she did not tick is unknown, not empty.
- **Check what a control actually measures before you build on it.** Three times
  in August a control meant something other than its label: the Tylenol counter
  was a running daily total, the one-tap meal button was a save time, and the
  "Water and salt" tile was two substances on one button.
- **The counters are running daily totals.** Last non-zero row of the local day
  IS the dose. Never sum rows, never sum sessions.
- **Her typed eating time beats every other time in the app.**
- **A supplement ask from her is a protocol change request.** Her K2 correction
  sat unread for five days. Action it or answer it the same day.
- **"Do not nag" limits YOUR guessing, not her asking.** On 26 August she asked
  in capitals to be kept on about her salt, one day after I told her I would
  stop. She is the patient.
- **One instruction, one source.** An instruction written in two places will
  contradict itself, and she follows the TILE, because the tile is where she
  taps.
- **Never let the app stop her recording the truth.** Tell her the rule, then
  let her log what actually happened.
- **Never ask her to eat less.** Eating more is the only thing that has moved
  her blood sugar.
- The Apps Script IDE rejects programmatic edits. Backend changes need his hands.
- `python` is not on PATH in git-bash here. `node` is.
- `claude/`, `FINDINGS.md`, `.claude/` and `_to_delete/` are gitignored. The repo
  is public and all four carry patient detail.
- `fh-state.json` IS published publicly. Flagged to Christopher, his call.
